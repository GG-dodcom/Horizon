## [OpenAI Data Shows Coding Agents Speeding Up AI Research](https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-view-inside-openai) ⭐️ 8.0/10

OpenAI published a research blog post titled 'Research acceleration: The view inside OpenAI', sharing early internal telemetry on how coding agents are used by its own research team. The data shows median daily AI spend per researcher rising from near zero in February 2026 to roughly $600 by late August 2026, with a steep jump in late July. This provides rare first-party evidence that agentic systems are already reshaping research velocity inside a frontier AI lab. It strengthens the case that coding agents and recursive self-improvement may compress the time needed for future AI advances, with industry-wide implications. Simon Willison, who highlighted the article, speculates that the late-July spending inflection corresponds to internal access to the model later released as GPT-6 Astra. The post accompanies another OpenAI essay, 'An Alien Mind' by chief scientist Jakub Pachocki, and together they have been widely described as signaling a recursive-self-improvement theme at OpenAI.

**Background**: Recursive self-improvement (RSI) is the concept of an AI system improving its own capabilities, potentially leading to rapid compounding progress toward advanced intelligence. Recent academic work distinguishes bounded self-refinement, already an industrial practice, from open-ended RSI, which remains constrained by grounding, collapse dynamics, and compute limits. Coding agents are AI tools that can autonomously write, debug, and modify code, and 2026 has seen them become a major productivity lever in software and research environments.

## [Animated Map Traces Los Angeles Building Boom (1880–2026)](https://lax-skyline.parcelscope.net/) ⭐️ 7.2/10

An interactive animated map visualizes more than a century of building construction across Los Angeles, from 1880 to 2026, using county assessor parcel data. The map reveals the city's growth block by block, from early streetcar-era development to today. The map offers an unusually granular, parcel-level view of urban development that can inform debates about housing policy and zoning. It has sparked rich discussion about how past downzoning and the rise of cars shaped Los Angeles's current housing crisis and built environment. The underlying data comes from the Los Angeles County Assessor's public parcel portal and reflects only structures that have survived to the present day. Consequently, neighborhoods like Palms that were later rebuilt appear understated, and demolished buildings are not shown, making older periods look emptier than they actually were.

hackernews · rustywasm · Sep 7, 18:52 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49601655)

**Background**: Assessor parcel data is structured property information compiled by county assessors, typically including an Assessor Parcel Number (APN), owner name, lot dimensions, land-use classification, and sale history. By joining these records with construction dates, the map can approximate when individual buildings were erected. Downzoning—reducing the allowed density of land—was heavily used in Los Angeles in the 1980s, and critics contend it created an artificial housing shortage by limiting new construction.
