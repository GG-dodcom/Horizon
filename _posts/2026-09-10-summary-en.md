## [OpenAI Claims a Lean 4 Formal Proof for the Navier-Stokes Problem](https://www.johndcook.com/blog/2026/09/09/formal-method-revolution/) ⭐️ 8.7/10

On 8 September 2026, OpenAI claimed a breakdown (counter-example) of Navier-Stokes solutions in three-dimensional Euclidean space and released an accompanying formalization in the Lean 4 proof assistant, reportedly generated by a swarm of around 10,000 AI agents running an internal frontier model. The result, which builds on a 2023 blowup technique by Diego Córdoba and Luis Martínez-Zoroa, has not yet been verified by external mathematicians or the Clay Mathematics Institute, and OpenAI said it will not claim the $1 million Clay Millennium Prize. If the proof survives external scrutiny, it would be a Millennium Prize-level result in mathematics produced largely by an autonomous AI agent fleet, marking a step change in what LLM-driven agentic systems can contribute to frontier research. It also forces the community to confront a new bottleneck: not generating proofs, but verifying them at acceptable cost and complexity. The result remains unverified by external mathematicians and by the Clay Mathematics Institute, and its announcement came with a priority dispute involving Levent Alpöge (employed at rival AI company Anthropic) and Tristan Buckmaster, who had derived closely related results on the Euler equations. Commenters also highlighted the practical cost of machine-checking: verifying the Lean formalization of Fermat's Last Theorem reportedly takes about 15 hours and 230GB of RAM, which is only roughly one order of magnitude faster than generating the Lean code took.

hackernews · ibobev · Sep 10, 21:22 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49650326)

**Background**: The Navier-Stokes equations are partial differential equations describing fluid motion; whether they always have smooth, globally defined solutions in three-dimensional space, or can break down, is one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems named by the Clay Mathematics Institute in 2000 and remains unsolved analytically. Lean 4 is an open-source proof assistant and functional programming language, released in 2021, in which mathematical statements are written in a formal language and every step is checked by a machine, so a proof accepted by Lean is verified independently of human reading. This is the essence of formal verification: proving correctness against a formal specification through mathematical rigor rather than informal peer review.

React Native is an open-source framework from Meta used by companies such as Facebook, Microsoft, and Shopify; Shopify's move is notable because React Native remains widely used and does not by itself imply the framework is failing. ## [Microsoft Designates Rust a Tier-1 Language](https://rustfoundation.org/media/guest-post-rust-is-tier-1-language-at-microsoft/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Microsoft has formally classified Rust as a tier-1 programming language, according to a guest post published by the Rust Foundation. The designation means internal teams get a "paved path" from local development to production, covering secure toolchain builds, productive developer tooling, quality workflows, deep platform integration, and compliance. This makes Microsoft the latest major OS vendor to diversify its systems-programming options for greenfield development, signaling that Rust is now treated as a mature, serious competitor to C++ rather than a fledgling language. Because around 70% of Microsoft's CVEs are memory-safety issues, the move could reshape how large-scale C/C++ codebases are migrated across its product portfolio. The tier-1 label is an internal engineering status rather than a specific product release, and publicly available details remain limited. Community members highlight that a notable technical development is Microsoft's adoption of the MSVC backend in place of LLVM, alongside an internal vision of converting 1 billion lines of code to Rust by 2030 with tooling targeting "1 engineer, 1 month, 1 million lines of code."

hackernews · mmastrac · Sep 10, 13:39 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49643546)

**Background**: Rust is a general-purpose programming language that emphasizes performance, type safety, concurrency, and memory safety, and it prevents entire classes of memory bugs such as null pointer dereferences and out-of-bounds access that are common in C and C++. Since memory-safety defects frequently become security vulnerabilities, memory-safe languages are considered more secure for systems software. Microsoft has historically maintained enormous C and C++ codebases in Windows, Azure, and its developer tools, which makes a shift toward a memory-safe language strategically significant.

## [Researchers question whether OpenAI can be trusted with unpublished math](https://mathstodon.xyz/@andreasthom/117240535270608201) ⭐️ 7.8/10

A Hacker News discussion drawing roughly 586 comments debated a Mathstodon thread by mathematician Andreas Thom asking whether researchers can safely share unpublished mathematics with OpenAI, after reports that OpenAI published results resembling ideas researchers had raised in chats with its models without crediting them. The thread also circulated claims about OpenAI generating 300 billion output tokens from a model still in training and about free model access extended to around 100,000 researchers, which commenters used to argue over contamination and attribution. The episode touches the core of how AI labs and academic mathematicians will collaborate: if shared ideas can reappear in a lab's publications without attribution, researchers lose the incentive to hand over unpublished work, and the credibility of claims that AI is cracking open problems erodes. It also sharpens a broader industry question about whether headline AI results on hard math and science benchmarks reflect genuine discovery or memorization of data the models were trained on. The technical crux of the dispute is whether an OpenAI result came from the model having seen the researchers' chats during pretraining (contamination) or from techniques discovered during reinforcement learning on verifiable math with large amounts of compute — commenters note both can be true simultaneously. Specific figures cited in the thread include roughly 300 billion output tokens generated by a model still in training and free access granted to about 100,000 researchers, though these numbers come from discussion rather than confirmed documentation.

hackernews · pred_ · Sep 10, 06:49 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49639408)

**Background**: Mathstodon is a Mastodon server popular with mathematicians (Terry Tao and others have written about joining it), so a thread there is a natural venue for this kind of academic complaint. "Data contamination" refers to test sets or problem solutions leaking into an LLM's training corpus, which can make a model look far more capable than it is; detecting it reliably is an open research problem. In LLM development, pretraining exposes a model to huge text corpora, while reinforcement learning (RL) later rewards it for producing verifiably correct answers, so a model can acquire both memorized facts and genuinely new problem-solving strategies. Academic norms, meanwhile, require attributing ideas to the people who contributed them — a norm that has no clear analogue when the "collaborator" is a proprietary model.

## [Calif Research claims AI helped build first zero-click WeChat worm](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/10/calif-research/) ⭐️ 7.5/10

Calif Research published a demo of WeWorm, which it describes as the first zero-click worm that spreads through WeChat calls on both iOS and Android, with the victim needing no interaction at all — not even answering the call. The team says that working with AI it found the underlying bug and wrote a first remote code execution (RCE) exploit in roughly two days, then built the worm in about one more week. If accurate, this is a striking data point for the emerging theme of AI-accelerated offensive security: work that a large team once needed months to complete was reportedly done by a small group in about a week. It suggests the bottleneck in exploit development is shifting from raw technical labor toward human judgment about what to target and how to test safely, which raises the tempo and scale of threats that defenders and platform vendors like WeChat must anticipate. The claim rests on a short promotional blockquote and a demo video rather than a published technical write-up, so the specific vulnerability, affected WeChat versions, and reproduction details are not disclosed. Calif Research frames the human role narrowly — providing judgment about targeting and safe testing — while attributing the bulk of the exploit and worm engineering to AI.

rss · Simon Willison · Sep 10, 00:56

**Background**: A zero-click exploit compromises a device without any action from the user, which makes it far more dangerous and valuable than attacks that require a click or a file download; a worm is malware that self-propagates from victim to victim, so a zero-click worm combines both properties and can spread with no human involvement. WeChat calls are a desirable vector because the app is used by well over a billion people and a call arrives as a trusted notification. Historically, finding such a bug and turning it into a reliable, cross-platform (iOS and Android) worm has been a months-long effort for skilled teams, which is why claims of AI compressing that timeline matter.

## [IBM Releases Granite Time Series PatchTST-FM-r2 Under Permissive License](https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-research/ibm-releases-sota-granite-time-series) ⭐️ 7.5/10

IBM announced the release of Granite Time Series PatchTST-FM-r2, a roughly 385-million-parameter zero-shot time series forecasting foundation model, as the successor to PatchTST-FM-r1. The model is dual-licensed under Apache 2.0 and the Linux Foundation's OpenMDW 1.0, making it commercially friendly for enterprise use. Time series foundation models aim to replace per-dataset model training with a single pretrained model that forecasts new series zero-shot, which could substantially cut the engineering cost of forecasting workloads in energy, retail, finance, and manufacturing. A permissively licensed, SOTA-class model from a major vendor lowers the legal and integration barrier for companies that cannot use restrictively licensed alternatives. The r2 model was trained on a diverse multi-domain corpus with a context length of 8192, a hidden dimension of 1024, a patch length of 16, and a quantile head spanning 99 quantiles, matching the configuration style of r1. Note that the available information is mostly the model card and release announcement, so independent benchmark comparisons against other time series foundation models are still limited.

rss · Hugging Face Blog · Sep 9, 15:36

**Background**: PatchTST comes from the ICLR 2023 paper "A Time Series is Worth 64 Words: Long-term Forecasting with Transformers." Its key idea is to split a time series into windows or patches that serve as input tokens to a Transformer, which retains local semantic information, quadratically reduces the computation and memory of attention maps for the same look-back window, and lets the model attend to longer history. Time series foundation models extend this line of work by pretraining on large cross-domain corpora so that a single model can forecast unseen series without task-specific training, a paradigm already pursued by models such as TimesFM and Moirai. IBM's Granite TimeSeries family is a productized effort in this direction, with r2 succeeding r1.

## [NASA's Decorrelation Stretch Technique Now Reveals Ancient Rock Art](https://spinoff.nasa.gov/Manipulating_Satellite_Photos_Now_Reveals_Ancient_Images) ⭐️ 7.3/10

A NASA spinoff article documents how decorrelation stretch — a multispectral image-enhancement method originally developed for satellite and planetary imagery — is now being applied to archaeological imagery to reveal ancient rock art and surface features that are invisible or nearly invisible to the naked eye. The accompanying Hacker News discussion added practical substance, linking the original technical paper (DecorrelationStretch.pdf) and sharing a working GIMP recipe for reproducing the effect on ordinary photos. It is a concrete example of space technology transfer, showing how a tool built for remote sensing of other planets becomes a lens for reading humanity's own past on the ground. It also matters pedagogically: the method forces practitioners to confront the fact that the way human eyes — or any optical sensor — render the world is not canonical, which is a core lesson in signal processing and sensor design. Decorrelation stretch works by suppressing the cross-correlation between spectral bands so that subtle color differences, which normally wash each other out, become visually separable; in practice it is often paired with contrast stretching such as auto-levels or histogram equalization. Limitations and caveats are worth noting: the NASA article itself is fairly short, the real technical meat lives in the linked paper and the comment thread, and at least one commenter reported failing to find hidden rock art at Angkor Wat using bandpass filters and cross-spectral differencing, possibly because of the wrong location and hurried shooting.

hackernews · gumby · Sep 10, 15:29 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49645437)

**Background**: Multispectral imaging measures light in a small number of spectral bands — typically 3 to 15 — rather than the three broad red/green/blue channels of an ordinary camera; hyperspectral imaging is the extreme case, using hundreds of narrow bands. Decorrelation stretch is a visual-enhancement technique that maximizes the differences between these bands so that distinct materials (vegetation, minerals, pigments, weathered rock) stand out in color. NASA and planetary scientists have long used such stretches on satellite and rover imagery, and the same mathematics applies to any multi-band image, including photographs of rock art shot through colored filters.

## [Stratechery: Apple's Faith in Apps Is Its Biggest AI Blindspot](https://stratechery.com/2026/the-iphone-duo-the-intelligent-personal-hub-apple-watch-audio-intelligence/) ⭐️ 7.2/10

**Background**: Digital goods sit in a legal grey zone: copyright law generally treats a download as a licence rather than a sale, while the first-sale doctrine — upheld for imported books in Kirtsaeng v. Wiley and applied to downloaded software in the EU — lets buyers resell lawfully acquired copies. Publishers therefore write end-user licence agreements that specify you purchase a licence, not the item itself. Many of those agreements also bundle mandatory arbitration and class-action waivers, which push disputes into individual proceedings and are usually only escapable through a short written opt-out window.
