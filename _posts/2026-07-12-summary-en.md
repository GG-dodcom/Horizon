A systematic comparison reveals that Claude Code consumes approximately 33,000 tokens for its system prompt and cache setup before processing any user input, whereas OpenCode uses only about 7,000 tokens, indicating a significant difference in token efficiency. This matters because for heavy users of agentic coding tools, token overhead directly translates to cost, and the fivefold difference can dramatically affect operational budgets and tool choice. The measurement was done by inserting logging between the tool and Anthropic's endpoint, capturing all requests and usage. The caveat mentioned is that the comparison may not capture qualitative task performance, and the author plans to update with deeper task analysis.

## [Shingles Vaccine May Reduce Dementia Risk](https://www.economist.com/leaders/2026/07/09/a-no-brainer-for-protecting-your-brain) ⭐️ 7.5/10

A UK natural experiment using an age-based eligibility cutoff for the shingles vaccine showed a reduced probability of dementia diagnosis over seven years following vaccination. If causal, this would offer a safe, widely available intervention to delay or prevent dementia, affecting millions at risk for Alzheimer's and other dementias. The study leveraged a policy where individuals just below the age cutoff were eligible for the vaccine while those just above were not, mimicking a randomized trial. Critics argue the association may be due to less hospital contact among vaccinated individuals, reducing incidental dementia diagnoses.

hackernews · saikatsg · Jul 12, 15:23 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48881874)

**Background**: Shingles (herpes zoster) is a painful rash caused by reactivation of the varicella-zoster virus. The recombinant zoster vaccine (Shingrix) is recommended for adults 50 and older. Previous observational studies suggested a link between vaccination and lower dementia risk, but were prone to confounding. Natural experiments like age-based cutoffs provide stronger causal evidence.

## [Math.tanh in Chromium 148 Reveals Underlying OS via Fingerprinting](https://scrapfly.dev/posts/browser-math-os-fingerprint/) ⭐️ 7.3/10

A blog post reveals that in Chromium 148, the implementation of Math.tanh differs across operating systems (macOS, Linux) due to varying math libraries, such as Apple's and glibc, allowing OS fingerprinting through subnormal floating-point number handling. This technique adds a new, subtle vector for browser fingerprinting that could be used alongside traditional methods like canvas or WebGL, potentially increasing user tracking without easy mitigation. The fingerprint relies on subnormal floating-point numbers, where different math libraries may produce slightly different results for Math.tanh on certain inputs; however, community comments note that newer glibc uses correctly rounded tanh from CORE-MATH, which may reduce reliability.

hackernews · joahnn_s · Jul 12, 21:12 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48884853)

**Background**: Browser fingerprinting collects subtle device or browser characteristics to identify users. Subnormal floating-point numbers are very small numbers near zero that some systems handle differently, creating detectable differences across platforms. Math.tanh is a JavaScript function computing hyperbolic tangent, and its implementation can vary across operating systems depending on underlying math libraries.
